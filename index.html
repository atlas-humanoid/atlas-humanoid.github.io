/ 01Overview

One Human Video Demonstration, One Prompt

From one human video demonstration and one prompt, with no robot demonstrations and no task-specific training in simulation, ATLAS completes the task the prompt names. An agentic harness, not a policy pretrained on robot data, links the agent and the whole-body tracker, and it is a part of each. It learns at test time what neither can know in advance: how this real robot executes a command, and the fine-grained task specification in this scene.

/ 02Method

An Agentic Harness

ATLAS divides the work by what each layer knows and when it learns it. System 2, the agent, knows the task before any session; System 0, the whole-body tracker, knows whole-body motion. The harness, a part of each, links the two and learns what only the running robot shows.

/ 06Details

Setup & Scope

Setup

Which whole-body tracker is used?

The frozen whole-body tracker is ScaleBFM, trained before any session to follow human motion and adapt to terrain; it carries the robot up the stairs. The agent, the fast system and the retry are the same on the stairs as on the floor.

What runs on the robot, and what off it?

The robot senses with its own head camera (RGB-D) and joint state; some flat-ground tasks also localize it with an external tracking system. The whole-body tracker and the fast system's actor run on the robot; the VLM, SAM 3 and the learner's updates run on a PC over a link.

Does a person do anything during a run?

No one teleoperates the robot. A person only resets the object, after a success or when it leaves the camera's field of view, and steps in if the robot walks out of the workspace.

Scope

Next we will carry the fast system past the grasp, into sustained contact, and reuse real-robot experience so a new task starts from what the robot already knows.
